Welcome to Module 3.
From the supplies inventory at your office to your family's
monthly budget, a spreadsheet keeps your lists neat and does
the math for you.
You'll learn to find your way around the sheet,
move with the keyboard, and type,
copy and reshape data without fear.
Remember the old ruled ledger? A spreadsheet is the same columnar notebook,
except it adds for you and never gets tired. Picture a binder.
The workbook is the whole binder: the file you save and email.
Each worksheet is one page, shown as a tab at the bottom.
And each cell is one box, where a column meets a row.
Here's the window. Excel is shown, but Google Sheets and LibreOffice Calc look
much the same.
The ribbon on top holds the commands. Columns use letters and rows use numbers.
Click the cell that says Fastener.
It gets a thick border: that's the active cell.
The Name Box tells you where you are:
A6. And the formula bar shows what's really inside the cell.
Like a classroom seat plan, column C,
row 4 points to exactly one cell:
C4. Letter first, then number.
A range is a block of cells.
Drag from B2 to D5, or click B2 and Shift
click D5.
We write it B2 colon D5,
and read it B2 to D5.
Three columns times four rows:
that's 12 cells.
Now, move without the mouse. Tab moves right and Enter moves down.
Here's the trick encoders love:
type across a row with Tab,
press Enter at the end, and you're back at the
start of the next row.
Control Home jumps back to A1.
Control End goes to the last used cell. And in a long attendance sheet,
Control Down Arrow reaches the last entry in one press.
Time to type. Click A6 and type Fastener.
Press Tab and type box of 50.
Tab again for the quantity, 6,
then the unit cost, 85, as a plain number with
no peso sign. Keep one value per cell.
Careful:
typing into a filled cell replaces everything.
To fix only part of it, press F2, or double-click the cell.
At home, start your budget with the months.
Type Jan in A2, then find the fill handle:
the small square at the bottom-right corner.
Drag it down, and the sheet continues with February,
March and April.
For numbers, type 1 and 2,
select both, and drag, so the sheet sees the step.
Double-click the fill handle to fill down beside existing data.
Envelopes also come in a pack of 100, so don't retype it.
Click B5 and press Control C.
A moving dashed border appears:
the marching ants.
Click B9 and press Control V.
Done.
Press Escape to stop the ants.
Control X cuts instead, and Control D fills down.
Careful: pasting over filled cells replaces them without asking.
Control Z is your friend.
Forgot an item, like correction tape?
Right-click the row number and choose Insert.
If a column is too narrow,
double-click the line between two column letters to AutoFit it.
And a cell full of hash signs isn't broken:
the column just needs more room. Your safety net:
Control Z undoes, Control Y redoes,
and Control S saves.
Quick check. You're in cell F9, at the bottom of the inventory.
What takes you straight to A1? Home, Control Home, Control End, or Escape?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
Control Home.
Plain Home only goes to the start of the row, A9.
You can also type A1 in the Name Box.
So now you can find your way:
workbook, worksheet, cell, the Name Box and the formula bar.
You can move and select with the keyboard. And you can build:
type, edit, AutoFill, copy, insert, and undo.
Save your inventory.
Next, in topic 3.2, we'll turn it into a clean,
readable table.
