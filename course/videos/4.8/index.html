Next · Try it with your own report
What if one report could become a slide deck and
a narrated video, in under half an hour?
Gemini Notebook does exactly that.
In this video, you'll learn where to find it,
how to feed it your own report,
and how to check what it makes before your section chief sees it.
Gemini Notebook used to be called
NotebookLM. Same tool, new name.
You give it your own sources:
a Google Doc, a PDF, a Word file,
a web link. Every answer shows a small number you can click to
see the exact sentence it used.
And the Studio panel turns your sources into decks,
videos, quizzes and more.
First, where is it?
Type the address notebook dot google dot com.
The old NotebookLM address still works.
Or search NotebookLM, and click the result from google dot
com. The side panel even says formerly known as NotebookLM.
Or open the Google apps menu, the nine dots, and click Notebook.
Then click Try Gemini Notebook, and sign in with your
Google account.
Click New notebook. Add sources:
upload a file from your computer, or pick one from Google Drive.
Search the file name, click it,
then Insert.
Our sample is the Administrative Section Year-End Report 2026.
Only add files you are allowed to share, never personal data.
Before you make anything, ask a question.
What are the three biggest results this year?
The answer lists them, each with a small number.
Click a number, and the exact sentence from the report
pops up. Three days down to one.
Thirty-eight reams down to twenty-one.
Correct.
Now the fun part. In Studio, click the pencil on Slide Deck.
Choose Detailed Deck or Presenter Slides,
and a length. Then describe it: a short deck for the section chief,
results with numbers, one idea per slide.
Click Generate now.
About five minutes later, it's ready.
And here it is. Nine slides, our own numbers.
The old way versus the new standard.
Three working days down to one. Thirty-eight reams down to twenty-one.
Clean titles, big numbers, one idea per slide.
You can present it right there,
or download it as a PDF or PowerPoint file.
Next, Video Overview.
Pick Explainer, the Whiteboard style, and a custom topic:
explain to new staff how our section went digital.
About fifteen minutes later, you get an eight-minute video,
with a voice, drawings and chapters, all from one report.
Play it, or download it.
Impressive, but now we check.
Slide five says the drop in lost memos was driven
directly by the naming rule. The report never says that.
Slide nine adds Q1 and Q2 dates that aren't in
the report either.
Fix them with Revise: pick the slide, type the change,
and generate the revised deck.
Quick check.
The video calls your new AI rule mandatory,
but the report only says adopt a simple rule.
Do you keep it, fix the brief and make it
again, add an AI caption, or cut the sound?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
fix the brief and make it again,
or don't use it.
So: find it at notebook dot google dot com.
Make it from your own report, through Studio.
And check it, claim by claim, before anyone sees it.
AI did an hour of work in minutes.
Your five minutes of checking make it true.
Try it this week, with a file you're allowed to share.
