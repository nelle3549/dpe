Adding Media Clips

Video, music and your own voice in a slide, and making sure they actually play on the day.

Good to know: a picture or a chart already counts as media. A short clip or a few seconds of narration can show what words alone can't.

The big idea

A media clip is the intermission number at a barangay program.

  • Book it: choose the right clip and insert it.
  • Rehearse it: trim it short and set when it starts.
  • Check the sound system: test on the real computer and online.
  • Get permission: use music and videos you are allowed to use.

What you can add

Four kinds of media clips

Video file

A clip saved on your computer, e.g. a 20-second phone video of the new filing room.

Online video

A YouTube video played inside the slide. Needs internet while presenting.

Background music

Soft music under a photo slideshow. Keep it quiet and short.

Narration

Your own recorded voice explaining a slide, for decks people watch on their own.

Ask first: does this clip say something my words and pictures can't? If not, leave it out.

Inserting video

From a file, or from online?

PowerPoint: Insert › Video

Google Slides: Insert › Video has a YouTube tab and a Google Drive tab. PowerPoint: Insert › Video › This Device or Online Videos. Sample clip.

  1. Open Insert › Video (PowerPoint: the Video button on the Insert tab).
  2. Online: search YouTube or paste a link (PowerPoint: Online Videos).
  3. From a file: pick your clip, then Insert (PowerPoint: This Device; Google: from Drive).
  4. The clip lands on the slide, with its playback settings beside it.

A file

Plays without internet, even in a brownout. Makes the file bigger.

Online

Keeps the file small. Needs internet; breaks if the owner removes it.

LibreOffice Impress: Insert › Audio or Video, files only.

Inserting audio

Background music or narration

PowerPoint: Insert › Audio

Microsoft PowerPoint for Mac. Sample slide and music. Windows has the same tabs.

Background music

  1. Go to the first slide → Insert › Audio › Audio on My PC (Mac: Audio from File). A speaker icon appears.
  2. Click the icon → Playback tab → Play in Background. Set Volume: Low. Music plays across slides and loops.

Narration for one slide

Insert › Audio › Record Audio, speak 20 to 40 seconds, stop, OK. Playback: Start In Click Sequence or Automatically.

Google Slides: Insert › Audio, from Google Drive (MP3 or WAV). Untick "Stop on slide change" to keep it playing. Impress: Insert › Audio or Video.

Rehearse it · keep only what you need

Trim a 3-minute video to the 30 seconds that matter

Google Slides: Format options › Video playback › Start at / End at. PowerPoint: Playback › Trim Video with green and red markers. Sample clip.

  1. Click the video → Playback tab → Trim Video (Google: the Format options panel opens at Video playback).
  2. Drag the green start marker and the red end marker (Google: type Start at 00:28, End at 00:58). Press play to check, then OK.
PowerPoint only: add a short Fade In / Fade Out (0.5 s) on the Playback tab so it doesn't start abruptly.

Trimming doesn't cut the original file, so you can widen it later. Impress has no trim: cut the file first, e.g. in Clipchamp on Windows 11.

Playback options

Decide how and when the clip plays

Google Slides: Format options › Video playback. PowerPoint: click the video, then the Playback tab (names in the table). Sample clip.

SettingUse it when…
Start: In Click Sequence / When Clicked On (Google: Play (on click))You present live and want to choose the moment. Safest choice.
Start: Automatically (Google: Play (automatically))The clip should begin as soon as the slide appears, e.g. a kiosk loop.
Play Full Screen · Loop · Rewind (PowerPoint)Fill the screen for a short demo · repeat for a lobby display · reset to the start after playing.

Google's third choice, Play (manual), waits for you to click the video itself.

File size

Embedded or linked?

Embedded (default)Linked
WhereInside the .pptxA separate file beside it
SizeBigger deckSmaller deck
RiskToo big to emailBreaks if the file moves
  • Shrink it: File › Info › Compress Media (choose 720p for most uses).
  • Too big to email? Gmail attachments stop at 25 MB; share a Drive link instead.
  • Linked? Keep the deck and its clips in one folder and copy the whole folder.

Check the sound system

Test on the real setup, not just your laptop.

  1. On the presentation PC: open the deck from your flash drive or Drive and play every clip.
  2. Speakers and volume: check the room speakers, or your headset if online.
  3. Zoom: Share Screen → tick Share sound before you click Share.
  4. Google Meet: present a Chrome tab (tab audio is included), or tick share system audio when sharing your whole screen on Windows.
  5. Plan B: a backup copy, and the clip's link ready to paste in the chat.

Get permission

Just because you can play it doesn't mean you may use it.

Safer choices

  • Your own recordings, voice and photos.
  • Stock media built into your app, e.g. PowerPoint's Stock Videos (Microsoft 365).
  • Free libraries with a clear licence, e.g. Pixabay. Read the terms.
  • A YouTube video played through Online Videos, with the title and channel shown.

Avoid

  • Popular songs from Spotify or YouTube as background music.
  • Downloading someone's YouTube video to embed it, without permission.
  • Movie or TV clips "because everyone shares them".
  • Clips of colleagues or the public without their consent.
Credit line habit: add a small caption, e.g. "Video: PowerPoint: Inserting Videos, LearnFree (YouTube)". Credit is good manners, but it doesn't replace permission.
