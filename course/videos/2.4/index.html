Next · 2.5 Page Layout and Printing Setup
Your chief says: invite all 40 staff to the workshop,
each letter with their own name. Copy,
paste and edit 40 times, and sooner or later,
Dear Maria lands on a letter for Pedro.
Mail merge does it in one go:
one letter, one list, and a personalised copy for every
name.
Think of loot bags for a kid's party.
You make one design for every bag: that's your letter, the main document.
The list of kids who are coming is your data
source, usually a spreadsheet.
And the blank name tag on each bag is the merge field.
The data stays in the list, so fix a name in the list once,
merge again, and every letter is corrected.
Start with the list.
In a spreadsheet, row one holds one header row:
Name, Position, Section and Email.
These become your field names.
Then one person per row, with no blank rows in
between, and one detail per column.
No title like List of Participants above the headers.
Use sample names, never taxpayer data.
Then save and close the file.
Open your letter and go to the Mailings tab.
Click Start Mail Merge, then Letters.
Next, Select Recipients, Use an Existing List,
and open the invitee list. Keep First row of data contains column headers ticked.
Then click right after Dear, and choose Insert Merge Field,
Name.
Type the space and the colon yourself. Repeat for Position and Section.
A field can appear more than once.
Now click Preview Results, and the fields turn into real
names. Here is record two:
Ana Reyes, Administrative Assistant.
Use the arrows to check the first, the last and one in the middle.
Look at the spaces and punctuation around every field.
Then click Finish and Merge, Edit Individual Documents,
All, and OK.
Word opens a new file with a letter for each
person, each on its own page:
Juan Dela Cruz, Ana Reyes, Pedro Reyes and Ana Villanueva.
Four rows became four letters.
With 40 rows, it's the same work. Save both files:
the main document with the fields, and the merged file with the names.
Quick check.
Your list has three names, but the merged file has
five letters, and two are blank.
Is the font too small?
Are there empty rows in the list? Are the margins wrong?
Or did you skip Preview Results? Think about it for a moment.
The answer:
empty rows in the list. Every row becomes a letter, even an empty one,
or one with just a space.
Three more errors are common.
Wrong names, or no names at all,
usually mean you picked the wrong sheet.
Numbers lose their format, so 1,500 pesos shows as plain
1500.
And dates may come out as 11 slash 19. The fix is simple:
reconnect to the right sheet, and type amounts and dates
as text in the sheet, or keep them in the letter.
At the office, use it for invitations,
certificates of participation after every seminar,
and notices from one approved template,
plus labels and envelopes.
At home, think of wedding or debut invitations with each
guest's name, letters to fiesta sponsors,
or a thank-you promo to loyal customers of your side business.
In Word, it's the Mailings tab. LibreOffice Writer, which is free, has Tools,
Mail Merge Wizard. Google Docs has it built in only on some paid
Workspace editions; free accounts need an add-on.
Ask your ICT focal person first,
because an add-on can read the sheet you connect.
So: three parts, the letter, the list and the fields.
A clean list with one header row and no blanks.
And preview before you finish.
