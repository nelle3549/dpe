AI assistants can now suggest formulas,
explain them, and spot trends in your spreadsheet.
That's helpful, but one rule runs through this whole video:
AI is a copilot, not a replacement for your judgment.
It drafts.
You verify.
And you sign.
Treat AI like a fast, eager new OJT. It's fast:
it drafts a formula in seconds. It's confident:
it sounds sure even when it's wrong. It's new:
it doesn't know your file, your office rules,
or what's confidential.
So you supervise, because your name is on the report.
Copilot in Excel opens from the Home tab,
if your Microsoft 365 plan includes it.
Gemini in Google Sheets has a side panel,
with the right Google plan.
Or a chat assistant in your browser works with any app, even LibreOffice Calc.
Use only AI tools your office allows.
A vague prompt, like give me a formula for my
inventory, makes the AI guess.
A specific prompt names four things:
your app, the column letters with their names,
which rows hold data, and what you want, in plain words.
It needs no real data at all. Headers are enough.
Here it is in action. You type:
I use Excel, row 1 has these headers,
data is in rows 2 to 9. I need the grand total in E10,
and how many items cost over 1,000 pesos.
The AI answers with SUM for the total,
and COUNTIF, a new function, for the count.
Paste it in, then run three checks.
First, two rows by hand:
row 2 is 25 times 245,
6,125.
Row 9 is 3 times 410,
1,230.
Second, click the cell and check the range:
E2 to E9, all eight items,
no TOTAL. Third, check against the total:
E10 shows 22,039 pesos.
And the COUNTIF gives 5: count them by eye. Verified.
AI is also great at explaining formulas you inherited,
and you share only the formula.
Ask it to explain this SUMIF in simple words.
It says it looks down the Unit column for ream,
and adds the Total Cost from the same rows.
Now check the claim:
the ream items are A4 and long bond paper,
6,125 plus 7,950, or 14,075 pesos.
To spot trends, share only the monthly totals,
and ask what stands out.
The AI says February fell 20.5 percent,
and March rose 40.7 percent.
Both are right, but you only know that if you
recompute each figure with your own formula.
And AI sees numbers, not reasons.
Why did March rise?
Ask the supply officer.
Sometimes AI invents a function.
It suggests equals
SUMVALUES of E2 to E9, and the cell shows a
NAME error. Some real functions are too new for older Office,
like XLOOKUP in Excel 2016.
So tell the AI your app and version.
To check, type equals SUM and read the pop-up list.
If the name isn't there, it doesn't exist.
Quick check. The AI says the grand total is equals SUM of
E2 to E8. Is it correct, does it skip row 9,
can SUM not add pesos, or should it use AVERAGE?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
it skips row 9, the envelope.
It gives 20,809 instead of 22,039.
That's why you check the range.
The rule that matters most:
it's fine to share column headers,
formulas, made-up sample rows, and totals that aren't confidential.
Never paste names, TINs, addresses, taxpayer returns or personnel records
into a public AI tool, not even as a screenshot.
That's the Data Privacy Act of 2012.
And at home, never paste a GCash or bank statement.
So: prompt well, with your app, columns and rows,
and headers only.
Verify always: two rows by hand, the range,
and the total.
And protect the data.
