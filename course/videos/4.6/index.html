Your slides now have text, pictures,
a chart and smooth transitions.
Now let's add sound and video.
A 30-second clip can explain what three slides of text
can't. But media is also the top cause of the awkward
moment when nothing plays.
So we'll insert clips, and make sure they work.
Think of a media clip as the intermission number at a barangay program.
When it works, it's the best part.
When the sound system fails, everyone waits.
So there are four jobs. Book it:
choose the right clip.
Rehearse it: trim it and set when it starts.
Check the sound system. And get permission.
You can add four kinds of media. A video file on your computer,
like a phone video of the new filing room.
An online video, played from YouTube inside the slide.
Background music under a photo slideshow.
And narration, your own voice, for self-running decks.
Ask first: does this clip say something my words can't?
To insert a video, go to Insert,
Video.
This Device plays without internet, even during a brownout,
but it makes the deck bigger.
Online Videos, where you paste a YouTube link,
keeps the deck small, but needs internet,
and breaks if the owner removes the video.
Google Slides has Insert, Video too.
For background music, like Lola's 80th birthday slideshow,
go to the first slide, then Insert,
Audio, Audio on My PC.
On the Playback tab, click Play in Background,
and set the volume to low. The music plays across slides and loops.
For narration, choose Record Audio instead,
and speak for 20 to 40 seconds.
Rehearse it:
keep only what you need. Click the video, then Playback, Trim Video.
Drag the green start marker and the red end marker,
so a 3-minute video becomes the 30 seconds that matter.
Add a short fade in and fade out.
Trimming doesn't cut the original file, so you can widen it later.
Next, decide how it plays.
For a live talk, set Start to When Clicked On,
or In Click Sequence, so the clip waits for you.
Automatically suits a self-running deck, like a lobby display.
You can also play it full screen,
loop it until stopped, or rewind it after playing.
Watch the file size.
A clip from This Device is embedded inside the deck,
so phone videos can push it past 100 megabytes.
Linked clips keep the deck light,
but break if the file moves. To shrink it, use File, Info,
Compress Media, and choose 720p.
Too big for Gmail?
Share a Drive link.
Then check the sound system.
Open the deck on the presentation PC and play every
clip. On Zoom, tick Share sound before you click Share,
or your audience hears silence.
On Google Meet, present a Chrome tab, which includes the tab's audio.
And keep a Plan B: a backup copy and the clip's link.
Finally, get permission.
Safer choices:
your own recordings, your app's stock videos,
free libraries with a clear licence, and YouTube played through Online Videos.
Avoid popular songs as background music,
even with credit, and downloaded YouTube videos.
At work, never film taxpayers, their documents or their screens,
and ask colleagues for consent.
Quick check.
On Zoom, your video plays, but participants hear nothing.
Do you compress it, share again with Share sound ticked,
change the transition, or set it to loop?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
share again, with Share sound ticked.
Zoom only sends your computer's audio when it's on.
To recap. Insert from a file for reliability,
or online for a small deck.
Trim it, start it on click, and compress before you share.
Test it on the real setup, and use only media you're allowed to use.
