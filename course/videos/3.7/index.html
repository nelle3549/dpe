Next · 3.8 AI-Assisted Data Analysis
A table of eight numbers doesn't speak to a busy section chief.
A chart does.
In this video you'll pick the right chart,
select the right cells, insert it,
and label it so anyone understands it in five seconds.
Which item cost the office the most in January?
In the table, you read all eight numbers.
In a bar chart, the longest bar jumps out:
long bond paper, 7,950 pesos.
A good chart answers one question.
Write that question first:
it becomes your title.
Three charts cover most office reports.
Use a column or bar chart to compare items or sections.
Pick a sideways bar when labels are long.
Use a line chart for a trend over time, like the monthly CEPALCO bill.
And use a pie chart only for parts of one
whole, with about six slices or fewer.
Step one:
select the data. You need two things:
the labels and the values.
Drag over A1 to A9, the item names with the header.
Then hold Control and drag over E1 to E9,
the totals.
Both cover rows 1 to 9. Leave row 10 out:
the TOTAL is not an item, and it would distort the chart.
Step two: with the data still selected, insert the chart.
In Excel, go to Insert, Charts,
Recommended Charts, or press Alt F1 for a quick default
chart.
In Google Sheets, Insert, Chart opens the Chart editor.
In LibreOffice Calc, Insert, Chart starts the Chart Wizard.
Wrong type? You can change it later.
Step three:
label it. The chart title says what, where and when.
Axis titles say what is measured, and the unit, like pesos.
Data labels show the exact amounts.
The legend is only needed for two or more series, like budget versus actual.
In Excel, the plus button beside the chart adds them.
The default text, Chart Title, does not count.
Here's a good Lab 3 target. Sort the table first, largest to smallest,
so the biggest cost comes first.
Then insert a bar chart, because the names are long.
Title it: January 2026 Office Supplies Cost by Item.
Delete the legend, since there is one series.
Bond paper together is 14,075 pesos,
nearly two thirds of the total.
Then tidy up.
Move the chart by dragging its border,
not the bars. Resize it from a corner handle until every label reads
clearly. To try another type, use Chart Design,
Change Chart Type.
And if the items land in the legend instead of
the axis, Switch Row, Column swaps them.
At home, a pie fits the family budget,
because every slice is part of one 20,250 peso whole.
Eight budget lines were grouped into six,
like rice and groceries into Food.
Select the category and amount columns,
with no total, and insert a pie. Show percentages as data labels:
food is the biggest slice, at 37 percent.
A TOTAL row in a pie always takes exactly half,
because it equals all the other slices combined.
An axis that starts at 6,000 makes long paper look
fifteen times bigger than A4, when it's only about 1.3
times.
Start bar axes at zero. And 3D effects make front slices look bigger,
so keep charts flat.
Quick check.
You select A1 to A10 and E1 to E10,
and insert a pie. One slice is exactly half.
Is long paper half of all spending, did the TOTAL become a slice,
do pies enlarge the first slice, or is the legend missing?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
the TOTAL row became a slice.
Select rows 1 to 9 only.
So: right question, right chart, right range,
real title.
Choose column or bar to compare, line for time, pie for one whole.
Select labels and values, and leave the TOTAL out.
Label it with a title, axis titles and data labels.
