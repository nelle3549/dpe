The big idea

A function is a ready-made formula with a name.

  • Like the "Cook" button on a rice cooker: you put in the rice and water, press one button, and it runs all the steps for you.
  • You give the function the cells (the ingredients). It does the adding, counting or comparing.
  • Same names in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice Calc.

The fastest way

  3. Check the range, then press Enter.

AVERAGE

The typical value: total ÷ how many

FormulaMeansResult
=AVERAGE(E2:E9)average total cost per item₱2,754.88
=AVERAGE(D2:D9)average unit cost₱250.38

Check: ₱22,039.00 ÷ 8 = 2,754.875, shown as ₱2,754.88 because of the 2-decimal format.

Blank vs zero: AVERAGE skips blank and text cells, but a 0 counts and pulls the average down.

FormulaResult
=COUNT(C2:C9)8 numbers
=COUNT(A2:A9)0 (item names are text)
=COUNTA(A2:A9)8 filled cells
Rule of thumb: count a number column with COUNT; count names or any filled cells with COUNTA.
Office use: COUNTA on a Name column tells you how many people signed the attendance sheet.

Why bother?

SUM vs adding cells one by one

=E2+E3+E4+E5+E6+E7+E8+E9=SUM(E2:E9)
LengthGrows with every row; 300 rows is unreadableSame length for 8 rows or 8,000
Typing errorsEasy to skip or repeat a cell (E6+E6)One range, easy to check
New row inserted insideNot included: total is silently wrongRange stretches to include it
Text cell in the range#VALUE! errorSkips the text cell (so check your types)
Checking by a reviewerMust read every addressReads one range: E2 to E9
Try it: right-click row 5 › Insert, and type a new item with a Total Cost. Watch which total changes and which doesn't. Then Ctrl+Z.

Watch out

Three classic mistakes, with the numbers they produce

Wrong range

=SUM(E2:E8) → ₱20,809.00

Stops one row short and misses the envelopes (₱1,230.00). Correct: ₱22,039.00.

Counting the total row

=SUM(E2:E10) in E12 → ₱44,078.00

Double the real total. =AVERAGE(E2:E10) → ₱4,897.56. Inside E10 itself it causes a circular reference warning.

Text in a number cell

C3 = "30 reams" → =SUM(C2:C9) gives 68, not 98

=COUNT(C2:C9) gives 7, and E3 shows #VALUE!, which spreads to the E10 total.

Habit that catches all three: click the result, read the range in the formula bar, and ask "does this cover exactly my data rows?"
