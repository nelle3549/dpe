Next · 3.6 Worksheet Management
You copied a formula, and now the whole column shows
zeros. Why?
In this video, you'll see how formulas point to other
cells, what happens when you fill them down,
and how one small dollar sign keeps a reference fixed.
It's the difference between a total you can trust and
one you can't.
Think of cinema seats.
A relative reference is a direction:
sit one seat to the right of your friend.
Move the friend, and your seat moves too.
An absolute reference is the seat printed on your ticket:
row F, seat 12.
In a formula, the dollar sign is that lock, not money.
Before the letter, it locks the column.
Before the number, it locks the row.
By default, references are relative.
Type equals C2 times D2 in E2,
then double-click the fill handle.
Now press Control grave accent to show the formulas.
E5 became equals C5 times D5:
each row uses its own quantity and unit cost.
The row numbers followed you down,
and that's exactly what we want for Total Cost.
Now a worked example:
12 percent VAT for every row.
The rate sits in one cell,
B12, below the table, so you can change it in one place.
In G2, type equals E2 times B12,
then fill down.
Row 2 is right: 735 pesos.
But every row below shows zero. Click G3 and read the formula bar:
equals E3 times B13.
The rate moved down too, onto an empty cell.
The fix: lock the rate.
In G2, type equals E2 times dollar sign B,
dollar sign 12, then fill down again.
Click G3: equals E3 times dollar B dollar 12.
The E part moved, and the locked B12 stayed.
Now every row is right, and the VAT total is
2,644 pesos and 68 centavos.
Check it a second way: 22,039 times 12 percent gives the same answer.
You don't have to type the dollar signs.
Click just after B12 in the formula.
Press F4 once for dollar B dollar 12.
Press it again, B dollar 12,
locking only the row.
Then dollar B 12, locking only the column,
and then back to plain B12.
On many laptops, hold the Fn key.
Excel for Mac uses Command T, and LibreOffice Calc uses Shift F4.
Mixed references lock just one part.
In a price table, column A stays, because it holds the unit costs,
and row 1 stays, because it holds the quantities.
Type equals dollar A2 times B dollar 1 once,
then fill right and down.
C3 is 85 times 5:
425 pesos.
Beginners rarely need these, and they're not graded in Lab
3.
The pattern is everywhere:
one input cell that many rows depend on.
At the office, each item's share of the budget is
its cost divided by the locked grand total:
A4 paper is 27.8 percent.
At home, a sari-sari markup of 15 percent in one
locked cell turns a 24-peso can of sardines into 27
pesos and 60 centavos.
Change the rate once, and every price follows.
Quick check. G2 holds equals E2 times B12.
You fill it down to G5. What does G5 contain?
E5 times B12, E5 times B15,
E2 times B12, or E5 times dollar B dollar 12?
Think about it for a moment. The answer: equals E5 times B15.
From G2 to G5 is three rows,
so every relative row number goes up by 3.
Filling down never adds dollar signs.
You add them yourself, with F4.
So, before you fill, decide what moves and what stays.
Relative references move with each row. Absolute references never move:
lock the one shared rate with F4.
Mixed references lock only the column or the row.
