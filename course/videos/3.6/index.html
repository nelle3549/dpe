Real office files grow:
January, February, March, then a summary for the quarterly report.
In this video you'll manage the sheets in one workbook,
link them to a Summary, and make long lists easy to use and print.
Picture a binder on the office shelf.
The workbook is the whole binder:
one file. Each worksheet is a tabbed divider,
with its own tab at the bottom of the screen.
And the Summary divider goes in front,
so the boss sees the totals first.
The tabs live at the bottom-left of the window:
Jan, Feb, Mar, Summary, and the plus button for a
new sheet.
Right-click any tab for the menu:
rename, tab color, move or copy,
hide and delete.
Double-click the tab to rename it.
Hold Control and click several tabs to group them and edit them together.
Build it in four moves.
Rename Sheet 1 to Jan and give it a colour.
Copy the Jan sheet twice: right-click, Move or Copy, and tick Create a copy.
Rename the copies Feb and Mar.
Then insert a new sheet called Summary and drag it
to the far left. Now every month has the same layout.
Hide takes a sheet out of view,
but anyone can unhide it in two clicks.
Delete removes it for good, and formulas that pointed to
it show a REF error.
Protect lets people see the sheet but not change your
locked formulas.
So hiding is not security:
never hide taxpayer details in a shared file.
On the Summary sheet, click B2 and type equals.
Click the Jan tab, click E10,
and press Enter.
B2 shows 22,039 pesos.
Read the formula: sheet name, exclamation mark, cell.
Do the same for Feb and Mar, then add a SUM and an AVERAGE:
64,209 pesos for the quarter.
Change a number on Feb, and the Summary updates on its own.
Long lists need three tools.
Freeze the top row with View,
Freeze Panes, so headers stay put in a long attendance
log. Control F finds a word, like envelope.
Control H replaces a spelling. But careful:
replacing long with legal changes three items,
not one. Find first, then replace.
Sorting answers the question: what costs us the most?
Click one cell inside the table,
then Data, Sort, by Total Cost,
largest to smallest.
Long bond paper comes first, at 7,950 pesos.
Never sort one column alone, or the rows get mixed up.
And leave the TOTAL row out of the sort.
Turn it on with Data, Filter. Click the arrow on Unit Cost,
then Number Filters, Greater Than, 300.
Three items stay:
folders, printer ink and envelopes.
The rest are hidden, not deleted,
so SUM still shows 22,039, while the visible rows total
6,110.
Clear the filter when you're done.
Before printing, preview first with Control P,
and count the pages.
Fit all columns on one page.
Use Print Titles to repeat row 1 on every page.
And choose landscape for wide tables, with the page number in the footer.
Preview once, print once.
At the office: supplies by month with a quarterly Summary,
or attendance with a tab per month. At home:
a household budget with a tab per month,
for rice, the CEPALCO bill and load,
plus a Year tab. Make January perfect, then copy it.
Quick check. The Summary shows equals Feb, exclamation mark,
E10.
A co-worker renames the Feb tab to February.
Does the Summary show a REF error,
update itself, turn to zero, or need retyping?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
it updates itself.
References follow a renamed sheet.
Only deleting the sheet gives the REF error.
So, organise the tabs like a binder.
Add a Summary that pulls each month's total and stays linked.
And the everyday tools:
freeze, find, sort whole rows, filter,
and fit to one page.
