Next · 4.3 Working with Text and Lists in Slides
In topic 4.1 you planned your Lab 4 deck on
paper. Now we open the app and turn those boxes into
real slides.
We'll demo in PowerPoint, and name where things are in
Google Slides and LibreOffice Impress.
Like driving any brand of jeep,
the controls are where you expect.
PowerPoint is on most office PCs, with commands in the Ribbon tabs.
Google Slides runs in the browser and saves to Google Drive automatically.
LibreOffice Impress is free, and opens PowerPoint files.
If you switch apps, check your fonts.
Learn five parts by name.
At the top, the Ribbon: tabs of commands.
On the left, the thumbnails:
every slide in order. Click to jump, drag to reorder.
In the middle, the slide pane, where you edit.
Below it, the notes pane, for notes only you see.
At the bottom, the status bar:
slide number, views and zoom.
And the File tab opens Backstage view, for saving and exporting.
Next, pick one theme for the whole deck:
Design, Themes, then Variants.
It sets colours, fonts and backgrounds on every slide at
once, so you get repetition for free. Choose a clean one.
Then give each slide a layout for its job: Home, Layout.
Title Slide first, Title and Content for a chart,
and Blank for one big photo.
Now build your skeleton.
Start with the Title Slide: My Digital Skills Journey, and your name.
Press Control M for a new slide,
and add four more, typing only the titles from your
paper. Control D duplicates a slide and keeps its layout.
Drag a thumbnail to reorder, and Control Z undoes a
wrong delete.
Save it as Lab 4, your name.
Each view has a job.
Normal view is where you build.
Slide Sorter shows all slides as tiles:
at home, it's perfect for putting reunion photos from oldest
to newest. Reading View plays the show inside a window,
to check it alone. And Presenter View shows you your notes,
the next slide and a timer, while the audience sees only the slide.
Here's a useful office trick. Keep a backup slide, like the full inventory table,
in the file. Right-click its thumbnail and choose Hide Slide.
In Google Slides, it's Skip slide.
The show skips it.
But if the section chief asks how you got the
total, type its number and press Enter, and there it is.
During the show, a few keys make you look like a pro.
F5 starts from the first slide,
and Shift F5 from the current one.
Space or the right arrow goes forward, the left arrow goes back.
A number, then Enter, jumps to that slide.
B blacks out the screen, and W makes it white.
And Escape ends the show.
On a laptop, F5 may need the Fn key.
Quick check.
Mid-presentation, the chief starts asking questions,
and you want everyone to look at you, not the slide.
Do you press Escape, B, F5,
or Shift F5?
Think about it for a moment. The answer: press B.
The screen goes black. Press B again to return to the same slide.
Before people arrive, press Windows P to choose Duplicate or
Extend, and run F5 once. Control S keeps the editable file.
To share, File, Export, Create PDF.
In Google Slides, it's File, Download.
A PDF looks the same on any phone,
so send it to the chief or the family group chat.
But videos won't play in it.
So: navigate the ribbon, thumbnails, slide pane and notes.
Manage with one theme, varied layouts,
Control M, Control D and hidden slides.
Present with F5, B and Escape,
then share a PDF.
