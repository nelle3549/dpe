Next · 4.4 Inserting and Manipulating Graphical Elements
Ever seen a slide that was one long paragraph?
You try to read, the speaker keeps talking,
and you remember nothing. Let's put text on slides the right way:
placeholders, bullets, numbered lists, readable sizes,
and speaker notes for the rest.
Think of a jeepney signboard:
Carmen, Cogon, Divisoria.
You read it in one glance. Nobody paints the whole route on the jeep.
Your slide works the same way: a few key words, read in seconds.
Your voice and your speaker notes carry the full explanation.
Every new slide comes with placeholders,
the dashed boxes that say Click to add title and
Click to add text. Click inside and type:
your title on top, your bullets below.
Placeholders follow the theme, so every slide lines up.
For a small extra, like a source line,
use Insert, Text Box.
Bullets have levels, and four keys control them.
Enter starts a new bullet. Tab, at the start of a line,
moves it one level in. Shift Tab moves it back out.
Shift Enter gives a new line without a bullet. Keep it to two levels:
a third deserves its own slide.
Numbers or bullets? Ask:
does the order change the meaning? Steps to paste a chart:
yes, so number them.
A list of supplies: no, so use bullets.
To switch, select the list and click Home,
Numbering.
A barangay event schedule at home? Number it too.
Size matters:
the back row must read it too. Titles at 36 to 44 points.
Body text and bullets at 24 to 32.
And 18 points is the smallest,
for labels and sources.
Watch out for AutoFit:
PowerPoint quietly shrinks text that overflows.
Below 24 points?
Cut words or split the slide.
To fix a wordy slide, make three moves. Cut to key words:
drop phrases like please be informed that.
Split what's left into three bullets, about six words each.
And if it's numbers or steps, turn it into a visual, like a chart.
A handy guide:
about six bullets of six words, at most.
Here's a shared drive reminder after those moves.
Seven rules became three:
name files by section, type and date.
Official files only.
Ask before moving files. Then select the bullets and choose Home,
Line Spacing, one point five.
Empty space shows where one idea ends.
Now a real Lab 4 slide.
The first draft was an 80-word paragraph about Module 3.
After the makeover, the title says something:
formulas now do my math. Three bullets:
total equals quantity times unit cost.
SUM and AVERAGE.
Bond paper costs the most. And one big number: 22,039 pesos, the grand total.
Where did the cut words go?
Into the speaker notes. Click Notes on the status bar,
or View, Notes.
Write cues, not an essay:
a number, a name, a question.
Only you see them in Presenter View. One warning:
on Zoom, share the slide show,
not your editing window, or everyone reads your notes.
These rules work everywhere.
At the office:
a numbered first-week list for new staff,
three bullets and one number for a briefing,
one reminder per slide on a lobby screen. At home:
your kids' school report, three bullets per slide,
or a sari-sari promo:
new, iced coffee, 25 pesos.
Quick check.
PowerPoint shrank your nine bullets to 14 points.
Do you make it smaller, cut and split into two
slides, switch to a narrow font, or let people zoom in?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
cut and split.
Trim to key words, use two slides,
and move details to your notes.
To recap.
Type into placeholders, with Tab for levels and numbers for
steps. Format for reading:
24 points or more, room to breathe,
and press F7 for spelling.
