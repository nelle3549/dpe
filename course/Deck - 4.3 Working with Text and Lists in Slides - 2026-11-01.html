At work: your section chief reads a slide in seconds. Today you'll see slides 2 to 5 of the demo deck filled with short, readable text.

Three verbs again: type, format, trim. Trimming is the most important skill in this topic, and the hardest, because we love our own words.

The goal is text and bulleted lists that look clean. Clean means short, aligned, consistent and readable. Ask: "Who has been told 'masyadong daghan og text' about their slides before?"

The big idea

A slide is a jeepney signboard, not a letter.

  • The signboard says "Carmen · Cogon · Divisoria". You read it in one glance from the sidewalk.
  • Nobody paints the whole route description on the jeep.
  • Your slide: a few key words the audience reads in seconds.
  • Your voice and your speaker notes: the full explanation.

In CDO we read jeepney signboards in one second while the jeep is moving. That's the standard for a slide: glance and understand.

Look at the photo: the audience is looking at the speaker. That's what we want. If the slide is full of sentences, they look at the screen and read ahead of you. Ask: "If the jeep had a paragraph on it, would you catch the right ride?"

Where text goes

Placeholders first, text boxes for extras

  1. Placeholders come with the slide layout: the dashed boxes "Click to add title" and "Click to add text".
  2. Click inside and type: the title on top, the bullets below. They follow the theme's fonts and positions.
  3. For a small extra, like a source line, use Text Box and draw a box anywhere. It only partly follows the theme.
Why it matters: titles in the title placeholder show up in the outline, help screen readers, and change together when you switch themes.

Demo: click inside a placeholder and type. Then Insert, Text Box, draw a small box and type a source line. In Google Slides it's Insert, Text box; in Impress, Insert, Text Box too.

A common beginner habit is deleting the placeholders and drawing text boxes everywhere. It looks fine at first, but the title is then invisible to accessibility tools and nothing lines up across slides. Ask: "Which one should hold your slide title?" The placeholder.

Bulleted lists

Move bullets in and out with Tab

  1. Enter starts a new bullet at the same level.
  2. Cursor at the start of the line, press Tab to demote it one level in. The buttons: Home › Decrease / Increase List Level.
Shift + TabPromote: move it back out Shift + EnterNew line without a new bullet
Rule of thumb: two levels at most. A third level deserves its own slide.

Demo on slide 3 of the demo deck: type "Word processing", Enter, then Tab to indent. Type two sub-points. Press Shift+Tab to come back out. PowerPoint also has Increase / Decrease List Level buttons on the Home tab; Google Slides has indent buttons.

Shift+Enter is the hidden gem: a line break that doesn't create a new bullet. Ask: "Can you see the third level on the left? How easy is it to read from the back of the room?" Not easy. Keep it to two levels.

Numbered lists

Numbers when order matters, bullets when it doesn't

  1. Steps to paste a chart: does the order change the meaning? Yes, so these bullets should be numbers.
  2. Select the list and click Home › Numbering, next to Bullets. Its arrow gives 1. 2. 3. or a. b. c.
Google SlidesToolbar Numbered list, or Format › Bullets & numbering
ImpressFormat › Bullets and Numbering

A list of supplies (bond paper, ballpens, printer ink) has no order: keep the bullets.

A simple test: would changing the order change the meaning? Steps to paste a chart: yes, so number them. A list of supplies: no, so bullets.

Demo: select a bulleted list and click Numbering on the Home tab. Show the arrow menu with other styles. Ask: "Your list of 'first-week tasks for new staff', bullets or numbers?" Numbers, because day 1 comes before day 2.

Font size

Body text at about 24 pt or bigger

36–44 ptSlide title
24–32 ptBody text and bullets
18 ptSmallest: labels, sources
Watch AutoFit: PowerPoint quietly shrinks text that overflows. If your bullets drop below 24 pt, cut words or split the slide. The AutoFit Options button even offers Split Text Between Two Slides.

These are app points on a normal widescreen slide. The default Title and Content layout already starts you at good sizes; problems start when we squeeze in more text.

Demo AutoFit: paste a long paragraph into a placeholder and watch the font shrink. Click the small AutoFit Options icon that appears and show "Split Text Between Two Slides". A fun rule popularised by Guy Kawasaki: font size should be about the age of the oldest person in the room, divided by two.

Spacing

Give your text room to breathe

  1. Seven rules fill the slide: too many lines to scan.
  2. Cut to three. The other four go in the notes or a printed reminder.
  3. Select the bullets, Home › Line Spacing › 1.5. Empty space shows where one idea ends.
Google SlidesFormat › Line & paragraph spacing
ImpressFormat › Spacing, or Sidebar › Paragraph

Line spacing between about 1.0 and 1.5, plus a little space between bullets, makes a big difference. Empty space is not wasted space; it tells the eye where one idea ends.

Notice the right slide also cut from seven rules to three. The other four can go in the notes, or on a printed one-page reminder. Demo: select the bullets, Home, Line Spacing, 1.5. Ask: "Which of the seven rules would you keep for your section?"

Trim the text

Three moves to break a wall of text

1

Cut to key words

Remove "please be informed that", "in order to", "the said". Keep nouns, verbs and numbers.

2

Split into 3 bullets

One idea per bullet, about 6 words each. Three is easy to remember.

3

Turn it into a visual

Numbers become a chart, steps become a diagram, places become a photo (Topic 4.4).

The 6 × 6 guide: about 6 bullets per slide and 6 words per bullet, at most. It's a guide, not a law, but it keeps you honest.

Three moves, in order. Cut first: government writing has many polite phrases that slides don't need. Then split what's left into bullets. If the content is numbers or steps, show it instead.

Where do the cut words go? Into the speaker notes and your mouth. Nothing is lost. Ask: "What phrase do we use in memos that we could cut from every slide?" Expect "Please be informed that" and "In connection with".

The speaker notes pane

The words you cut go here

  1. Open it: status bar › Notes, or View › Notes. Google Slides: under the slide. Impress: View › Notes.
  2. Write cues, not an essay: numbers, names, questions to ask. "Bond paper is ₱14,075 of ₱22,039. Ask: buy in bulk?"

Print them as Notes Pages, or read them in Presenter View. Only you see them.

This is exactly what I'm doing right now: you see the slide; I see these notes in presenter mode. Demo by opening the notes pane on a demo deck slide and typing a cue.

To print: File, Print, change "Full Page Slides" to Notes Pages. Warn them: write notes as cues, not a script you read word for word. Reading sounds robotic. Ask: "What's one cue you'd write for a chart slide in your next briefing?"

Aa Capitalisation

Pick one style for titles and one for bullets, then stick to it.

Sentence caseFormulas now do my math
Title CaseFormulas Now Do My Math
ALL CAPSFEELS LIKE SHOUTING

PowerPoint: Home › Change Case (Aa), or Shift+F3 to cycle. Google Slides: Format › Text › Capitalisation.

A spelling error on a slide projected for 30 people is very visible, especially in an official briefing. Run F7 before every presentation.

On capitals: Sentence case is easiest to read and is the modern default; Title Case is fine if you're consistent. Avoid ALL CAPS for anything longer than two or three words. Demo Shift+F3 on a selected title: it cycles through lowercase, UPPERCASE and Capitalize Each Word.

Quick check · click an answer

PowerPoint shrank your 9 bullets to 14 pt so they fit. What's the best fix?

A
Make it 12 pt so it fits better
No. Smaller text is even harder to read, especially on a phone or projector.
B
Cut to key words, split into two slides, move details to the notes
Yes. Keep body text around 24 pt or more; slides are free.
C
Change to a narrow, condensed font
No. Squeezed letters are harder to read; the problem is too many words.
D
Leave it; people can zoom in
No. Audiences in a room can't zoom, and online viewers won't bother.

Give them a minute to answer in the portal, then click the options to reveal. The answer is B.

Remind them of the AutoFit Options button that offers "Split Text Between Two Slides" as a quick first step. Then they still need to trim each bullet. Ask: "Which kind of slide is most at risk of this?" Usually the "Background" or "What I learned" slide.
