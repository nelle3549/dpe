Selecting text

Mouse or keyboard, pick your favourite

🖱️

With the mouse

Click, don't drag
  • Double-click: one word
  • Triple-click: a paragraph
  • Click, then Shift+click: a long stretch
⌨️

With the keyboard

Hold Shift and move
  • Ctrl+Shift+→: word by word
  • Shift+End: to the end of the line
  • Ctrl+A: everything
Shift means stretch: hold it with any movement key.

In topic 2.1 we pasted our plain invitation letter into
the word processor and saved it.
Now we make it look like a real formal letter,
with the right font, alignment, spacing and lists.
Then we learn the editing tools that save the most
time.
The most important habit:
first select, then tell it what to do.
It's like a highlighter on a printout.
You mark the lines first, then decide what to do
with them. Selected text turns light blue, and every command acts only
on the selection.
And if nothing is selected, most commands act on the
word where the cursor sits.
You can select faster than shaky dragging.
With the mouse, double-click a word,
or triple-click a paragraph.
For a long stretch, click at the start,
then hold Shift and click at the end.
With the keyboard, Shift means stretch:
hold it and move.
Control Shift and the right arrow selects word by word,
and Control A selects everything.
Press Control A and pick one clean font for the
whole letter, like Arial or Calibri.
Body text stays at 11 or 12 points.
Now select the office name. On the Home tab, make it bold,
14 point, and centered.
Center the section name too. Then make the subject line bold with Control B.
When everything is bold, nothing stands out.
Next, paragraphs.
Use Center, Control E, for titles,
never the spacebar.
Now select the body paragraphs and press Control J to
justify. Look at the right edge:
straight edges on both sides, like a newspaper column.
Then set line spacing to 1.15,
with 8 points after each paragraph.
That beats pressing Enter twice.
Three tools stop you repeating yourself.
For lists, use bullets when the order doesn't matter,
and numbers when it does, like the steps to register.
Don't type the bullets yourself.
A style is a saved bundle of formatting,
like an office uniform:
change it once and every heading updates.
And the Format Painter copies the look,
not the words. Double-click the brush to paint many places.
Everyone knows Control C and Control V.
Here's the new skill.
Paste a line from an email, and it arrives in a different font,
size and colour. Don't retype it.
Click the small clipboard tag, or
right-click, and choose Keep Text Only.
Now it matches your letter. In Google Docs, use Control Shift V.
And for a messy paragraph, select it and click Clear
Formatting.
A word wrong all over? Press Control H.
Find what:
apps.
Replace with applications.
Tick Find whole words only, or replacing Ana with Anna
also changes Analysis. Made a mistake?
Control Z undoes it.
Then proofread.
A red wavy line means a possible misspelling.
Right-click it and choose Colleagues, or press F7 to check the whole letter.
Names like Pabellan get flagged too: choose Ignore.
At the office:
a memo with a justified body and a bold subject.
A notice to taxpayers with numbered steps and the deadline
in bold. A report from many contributors:
paste as text only, then one Format Painter pass.
At home: the family reunion notice, with a centered title and a
bullet list.
Or the sari-sari price list:
when prices go up, Find and Replace.
Quick check.
Spell check shows no red lines in your letter. Is the letter error-free?
Are the dates and names correct? Is the tone right?
Or does it only mean each word is a real word?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
only that each word is real. Form instead of from passes.
So does a wrong date. You are the final proofreader.
So, select first, then format with the right tool:
one font, a justified body and even spacing.
Edit cleanly with Keep Text Only,
undo and Find and Replace.
And proofread it yourself. Next, in topic 2.3, we add a schedule table and
a logo to our letter.
