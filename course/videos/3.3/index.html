In this video, you'll write formulas that add,
subtract, multiply and divide, and update themselves.
At the sari-sari store, the tindera punches the numbers,
writes the total, and forgets how she got it.
A receipt is a frozen answer.
A formula keeps the recipe:
quantity times unit cost.
Change an ingredient, and the answer changes too.
The cell shows the answer, and the formula bar shows the recipe.
Rule number one:
every formula starts with an equals sign.
Without it, the sheet just shows what you typed,
as text. Click E2 and type equals.
Then click C2, type the star for multiply,
click D2, and press Enter.
The cell shows 6,125 pesos.
Clicking the cells avoids typing a wrong address.
Spreadsheets use six symbols.
Plus adds: C2 plus C3 is 55 reams.
Minus subtracts:
25 minus the reorder level of 10 is 15.
Star multiplies.
Slash divides:
a box of 12 ballpens at 96 pesos is 8 pesos each.
The caret is for powers, rare in office work.
And parentheses tell the sheet what to do first.
Just like in school, multiply and divide go before add
and subtract.
So 2 plus 3 times 4 is 14, not 20.
Now average the rice budget and actual.
Without parentheses, the sheet divides only the actual and shows
3,825, bigger than both amounts.
With parentheses, you get 2,575.
When in doubt, add parentheses.
Here's the most important habit.
E2 holds equals C2 times D2.
Now the supplier raises the A4 price, and D2 changes to 255.
E2 updates by itself to 6,375 pesos.
If you had typed equals 25 times 245,
it would still say 6,125:
wrong, with no warning.
In Lab 3, every calculated cell must point to cells.
Now build the whole column. Click E2 again, then double-click the fill handle.
One formula becomes eight, and each row points to its
own cells. Click E5 and read the formula bar:
equals C5 times D5.
The row number changed by itself. Check two answers:
folders, 2,080 pesos, and printer ink,
2,800 pesos.
Now the grand total.
Type TOTAL in A10.
In E10, add the eight totals with plus signs:
equals E2 plus E3, all the way to E9.
Press Enter:
22,039 pesos.
Long, isn't it?
In topic 3.4, one short function does the same.
Tip:
Control grave accent shows every formula,
so you can spot a total typed by hand.
At home, compare your budget with what you spent.
In D2 type equals C2 minus B2,
and fill it down.
Positive means over budget:
the CEPALCO bill went 320 pesos over.
Negative means you saved.
In E2, divide actual by budget and apply Percentage format:
111.4 percent for CEPALCO.
Don't multiply by 100; the format does that.
Errors are clues, not disasters.
A div zero error means you divided by zero or an empty cell.
A value error means math on text, like 30 reams.
A name error means the sheet doesn't know a word,
like the letter x instead of the star.
And hash signs just mean the column is too narrow.
Quick check.
Which formula correctly computes the total cost of folders in E5?
Equals 4 times 520, equals C5 times D5,
C5 times D5 with no equals sign,
or equals C5 x D5?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
equals C5 times D5.
The first one is right today, but it won't update when the price changes.
To recap: type the data, and let formulas do the math.
Write them with an equals sign and the six operators.
Reference cells, then fill down, so every total follows a
price change.
And check your work.
