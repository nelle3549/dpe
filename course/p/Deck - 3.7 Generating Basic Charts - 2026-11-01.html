Why charts

A good chart answers one question at a glance.

  • Compare: which item, section or month is biggest?
  • See a trend: are supply costs going up or down?
  • Show a share: how much of the budget goes to food?
  • Spot the odd one: a month that suddenly jumps stands out.
Rule of thumb: before you make a chart, write the one question it should answer. That question becomes the title.

Pick the right chart

Three charts cover most office reports

Column / Bar · compare

Items, sections or people side by side. Use a bar (sideways) when the labels are long.

Line · trend over time

Months, weeks or days in order. Shows rises, dips and seasons.

Pie · parts of one whole

Shares that add up to 100%. Keep it to about 6 slices or fewer.

Step 1 · select the data

Labels + values only. Leave out the TOTAL row.

  1. Drag over A1:A9 (the item names, with the header).
  2. Hold Ctrl and drag over E1:E9 (the totals). Mac: Cmd.
  3. Check: both selections cover the same rows, 1 to 9.
Row 10 stays out. The TOTAL is not an item; including it distorts the chart.

What we're aiming for

A bar chart the boss reads in five seconds

  • A title that says what, where and when.
  • Sorted largest to smallest (sort the data first).
  • Data labels show the exact peso amounts.
  • No legend needed: there is only one series.

Step 3 · label it

The parts of a chart

  1. Chart title · required in Lab 3
  2. Axis titles · what and which unit (₱)
  3. Data labels · the numbers on the bars
  4. Legend · only when there are 2+ series

Excel: the + button beside the chart. Sheets: Chart editor > Customize. Calc: double-click the chart, then Insert.

Tidy up

Move it, resize it, change its type

Move

Drag the chart by its border, not the bars. Place it beside the table, not on top of the data. Excel can also Move Chart to its own sheet.

↘

Resize

Drag a corner handle. Make it big enough that every label is readable, without overlapping text.

Change type

Excel: Chart Design > Change Chart Type. Sheets: Chart editor > Setup > Chart type. Calc: right-click the chart in edit mode > Chart Type.

Delete the legend when there is only one series: click it and press Delete. Less clutter, same meaning.

Budget vs Actual

Two series? Now you need the legend.

  • Select three columns: Item, Budget, Actual.
  • A clustered column puts each pair side by side.
  • The legend tells green (Budget) from gold (Actual).

Groceries went ₱480 over, CEPALCO ₱320, fare ₱240, rice ₱150. Total: ₱21,210 against a ₱20,250 budget, ₱960 over.

Line chart · trend

Months in order, one line

  • Time goes on the horizontal axis, left to right.
  • The line shows the summer peak in April and May, then a drop in June.
  • Axis titles say what and which unit.

Common mistakes

Three charts that tell the wrong story

TOTAL row in the pie

The TOTAL slice is always exactly half the pie. Select rows 2 to 9 only.

Axis starts at 6,000

Starts at 6,000: looks 15× bigger

Starts at 0: about 1.3× bigger

Bar and column axes should start at zero.

3D effects

Tilting makes front slices look bigger than they are. Use flat 2D charts.

Also avoid: more than ~6 pie slices · "Chart Title" left as is · no axis titles · a pie of unrelated numbers.

See it everywhere

Match the chart to the question

At the office
  • Bar: supplies cost by item, for the property or supply officer.
  • Column: staff present per section, from the attendance sheet.
  • Line: documents received per week over a quarter.
  • Pie: share of this month's supplies budget by category.
At home
  • Pie: where the monthly budget goes.
  • Line: the CEPALCO bill month by month.
  • Column: sari-sari sales per day of the week. Is Sunday the best?
  • Clustered column: budget vs actual for the fiesta handaan.
