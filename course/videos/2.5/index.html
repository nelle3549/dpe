The signature block alone on page two.
A file formatted for A4, printed on long bond.
Thirty pages when you needed one.
None of these are printer problems.
They're layout problems, and each one takes less than a
minute to fix before you click Print.
Think of framing a graduation photo.
Paper size is the size of the frame.
Margins are the white border inside it.
Orientation is whether you hang it tall,
in portrait, or wide, in landscape.
And the header and footer are the label on top
and the caption below, repeated on every page.
Click Layout, Margins, Normal.
That's one inch on all four sides,
the Lab 2 requirement.
If your ruler shows centimetres, one inch is 2.54.
Next, match the paper in the file to the paper in the tray.
Careful:
long is not Legal.
Legal is eight and a half by fourteen.
If long isn't listed, use More Paper Sizes and type
eight and a half by thirteen. Keep letters in portrait.
Headers and footers repeat on every page.
Double-click the top of the page and type the office name.
Then click Insert, Page Number, Bottom of Page,
and pick Page X of Y for longer documents.
Never type page numbers by hand: they break the moment you add a paragraph.
And if page one carries the letterhead,
tick Different First Page.
To start a new page, like Annex A for the schedule, press Control Enter.
That's a page break, and it works in Word,
Google Docs and LibreOffice.
A section break goes further:
one section can be landscape for a wide table.
Don't press Enter fifteen times.
Everything shifts when you edit above,
and the signature ends up alone.
Turn on Show Hide to see the breaks.
Press Control P. The preview is free; paper and ink are not.
Check the page count, the signature,
the table width and the numbers.
Then print only what you need.
Type one to three, comma, five for those four pages.
Three copies for three signatories?
Choose Collated for complete sets.
Print on both sides if the printer can,
and Scale to Paper Size fits an A4 file on long bond.
Emailing or uploading instead?
Save a PDF. Fonts, margins and page breaks stay exactly as you set
them. In Word, File, Save As, PDF.
In Google Docs, File, Download, PDF.
In LibreOffice, Export as PDF.
And any app on Windows can print to Microsoft Print to PDF.
Keep the Word file as your editable master.
Quick check. You need pages two, three and five only,
in three complete sets for three signatories.
Which settings?
Think about it for a moment.
The answer:
B. Pages two to three, comma five,
three copies, collated.
Option A also prints page four, and uncollated means sorting by hand.
At the office, preview first and print one test copy
before the batch.
Print drafts double-sided in grayscale, and email the PDF instead
of copies just to read.
Reuse scrap paper only if it has no personal or
taxpayer data; shred the rest.
At home, print only the pages of the kids' modules
you need, use grayscale for worksheets,
and keep bills as PDF.
Before a letter goes to the chief's table, check five things.
The right paper and 1-inch margins.
Names and positions spelled right, with the correct date.
No placeholders like Insert Name.
The signature stays with its paragraph. And the right number of copies,
including the receiving and file copy.
So, set up the page first.
Use headers, automatic page numbers and breaks.
Preview always, and print only what you need.
