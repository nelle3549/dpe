Four steps. We start with something many people skip: where to find the tool and how to open it. Then we add the report, ask a question, and make the two outputs. We end by checking them, because the AI got a few things wrong, and I'll show you exactly where.

If you want to follow along, download the practice file now and put it in your Google Drive. You need a Google account; a personal Gmail works.

The big idea

An AI that reads only your sources.

  • Gemini Notebook is Google's notebook for your own files. Its old name was NotebookLM.
  • Sources: Google Docs and Slides, PDFs, Word files, web links, YouTube videos, pasted text.
  • Answers cite the source: click a number to see the exact sentence it used.
  • Studio turns the sources into a slide deck, a video, an audio talk, a quiz, flashcards and more.
  • Still a copilot: it can add things your source never said.

The key difference from a normal chatbot: Gemini Notebook answers from the sources you give it, and it shows where each answer came from. That makes checking much faster.

But "from your sources" does not mean "always right". Today's deck and video both added claims our report never made. We'll find them together.

If someone asks: yes, NotebookLM and Gemini Notebook are the same product. The old address still works; it now opens the new name.

Microsoft Edge on a Mac, sample Google account. Any browser works: Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari.

Step 2 · Your source

One notebook per project

  1. On the home page, click New notebook.
  2. Upload files from your computer, or pick Drive. Links and copied text work too.
  3. In Drive, search the file name.
  4. Click the file, then Insert. It appears under Sources in a few seconds.

One notebook per project: one for the year-end report, another for a training, another for a policy. Each notebook only sees its own sources.

Step 4 · Check every claim

Two slides say more than the report

Sample output: the 9-slide deck

Slide 5 · a cause it made up

"driven directly by our strict adoption of the new date-first naming rule". The report gives the numbers, not the cause.

Slide 9 · dates it made up

"Q1" and "Q2" are not in the report. Only the scanning has a date: June 2027.

Checked and correct: 3 to 1 working days, 38 to 21 reams (a 44% cut), 1 in 5 to 1 in 50, 2 to 14 staff, 1,240 memos, ₱22,039.00 across 8 items.

Read slide 5 aloud. It sounds reasonable, and that's the danger. Our report says the memos were renamed and that fewer are hard to find. It never says one caused the other. If the section chief repeats this in a meeting, it becomes a claim nobody can prove.

Slide 9 adds quarter labels. The report only says "replace the two oldest computers" and "a refresher every quarter"; the only date is June 2027.

Everything else checked out, including the 44% (38 minus 21 is 17; 17 out of 38 is about 44.7%). So the rule from 4.7 holds: keep the structure, fix what is untrue or unproven.

Revise works slide by slide. The deck stays as it is until you click Generate revised deck.

Fix, then share

Revise one slide, keep the rest

  1. Open the deck large and click Revise.
  2. Pick the slide, type the change: "Remove the sentence about the cause. Keep only the numbers." Then Generate revised deck.
  3. Present right here, or share a link.
  4. ⋮ › Download as PDF or PowerPoint.
Good to know: in the PowerPoint download, each slide is one picture. For small text fixes, use Revise, or rebuild that one slide yourself.

Revise is the fix. You choose the slide and say what to change, in plain words. Only that slide changes; the other eight stay. Show the typed request on screen: we tell it exactly what the report does not support.

Then download. Important: the PowerPoint file has each slide as a single picture, so you can't click into the text and edit it in PowerPoint. Fix it in Revise first, or rebuild the one slide in PowerPoint with the 4.2 template.

A shared link only works for people you add, like sharing a Google Doc.

What we got · then we checked it

Right numbers, a few extra words

  • All four results, the 1,240 memos, 120 offices, ₱22,039 across 8 items: correct.
  • "Reduces our environmental impact" and "frees up office space": not in the report.
  • Calls the AI rule "mandatory". The report says "adopt a simple rule".
  • Says AI tools "aren't invaluable". It meant "not perfect". Narration can misspeak.

Play the clip with sound: it's the results part, paper and response time. The voice and drawings are made by the AI from our one report.

Then the checklist on the right. The numbers are all right. But listen for extra claims: environmental impact and office space are nice ideas, not in our report. And one wrong word: the narrator says AI tools "aren't invaluable", which means the opposite of what it wanted to say.

A video is harder to fix than a deck: there is no Revise for one sentence. If a claim is wrong, change the custom topic and make it again, or don't use the video.

Before you use it at work

Six rules for Gemini Notebook

Only shareable files

No names, addresses, ID numbers, salaries or case details of real people. Check your office's AI policy.

Ask, then generate

A question in the chat shows quickly whether it understood your source.

Click the citations

Every number on a slide should match a sentence in your source.

Hunt for extras

Causes, dates, praise and "mandatory" words the source never said. Fix them with Revise.

Mind the limits

Free accounts have a daily AI usage limit. Decks take minutes, videos longer.

You own it

Your name is on the report. Label AI-made media when you share it.

Go card by card, quickly. The first one matters most: only files you're allowed to share. Gemini Notebook is an online service; treat every upload like an email to an outside office.

"Hunt for extras" is today's new skill. The AI fills gaps with words that sound right: a cause, a quarter, a "mandatory". Read every slide asking: "Where does it say that in my report?"

On limits: our demo account was almost out of AI usage after one deck and one video. Plan your important deck early in the day.

Space or K plays and pauses (or click the video). C turns captions on or off. The arrows still move between slides, and the video pauses when you leave this slide.
