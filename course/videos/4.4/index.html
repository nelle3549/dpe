In topic 4.3, we cut our words down.
Now let's replace some of them with pictures,
shapes, icons, diagrams and charts:
how to insert them, arrange them neatly,
and use them legally, including the Lab 3 chart for
your Lab 4 deck.
Picture two slides with the same Lab 3 numbers:
a table of eight totals, and a bar chart.
Which shows the biggest supply cost faster?
The chart.
The longest bar jumps out. A table is better when people need exact figures.
Either way, graphics should explain, not decorate.
Start on your Where I started slide. Go to Insert, Pictures, This Device,
and pick your Lab 1 image.
You can also drag a file from File Explorer onto the slide.
For a screenshot, press Windows Shift S,
then Control V on the slide.
Now resize it.
Drag a side handle and the photo stretches:
faces look wide, and the office seal looks wrong.
Drag a corner handle instead, and the shape stays the
same. Hold Shift to be extra safe.
Already stretched?
Use Picture Format, Reset Picture and Size,
or just Control Z.
Next, crop out what doesn't help.
Click the picture, then Picture Format,
Crop, and drag the black handles inward.
Crop to Shape gives you a circle for a profile photo.
Phone photos are big, so use Compress Pictures and choose
Web or E-mail. Gmail attachments stop at 25 megabytes.
Shapes and icons point and explain.
Use Insert, Shapes for arrows, boxes and callouts.
Click a shape and type to label it.
In Microsoft 365, Insert, Icons lets you search for a
printer or an envelope. Keep one style:
all line icons or all filled, in your theme colours.
Got a list of steps?
Make it a diagram.
Type your four modules as bullets: computers and internet, word processing, spreadsheets,
presentations.
Select them, then Home, Convert to SmartArt,
and pick a Process.
Use a Cycle for something that repeats,
like a monthly report.
Google Slides has Insert, Diagram.
For Lab 4: in Excel, click the edge of your Lab 3 chart
and press Control C. In PowerPoint, go to your chart slide and press Control
V. Paste Options lets you keep it editable or paste it
as a picture.
Rewrite the title as a message:
bond paper is 64 percent of our supplies cost.
Then right-click,
View Alt Text.
When you hang family photos at home,
you use a level and a tape measure. Align and Distribute do that job.
Shift-click three objects, choose Align Middle,
then Distribute Horizontally.
Control G groups them so they move as one.
Lost a shape behind a photo? Open the Selection Pane with Alt F10.
Google finds pictures.
It doesn't give permission.
Your own photos are safest.
Free photo sites like Unsplash, Pexels and Pixabay work under
each site's licence, and PowerPoint has built-in stock images.
And a recognisable face is personal data under the Data
Privacy Act: never use taxpayers' photos, or a screenshot showing a TIN.
At the office, show the Lab 3 chart with a
message title in a briefing, and a SmartArt of how
a document moves for new staff.
At home, crop and compress old photos for a reunion
slideshow, or chart the household budget against actual spending,
CEPALCO bill included.
Quick check.
You enlarged a group photo, and now everyone looks wide and short.
Was it compressed too much, does it need alt text,
did you drag a side handle, or is the picture style wrong?
Think about it for a moment. The answer:
you dragged a side handle.
Undo, then use a corner.
To recap. Insert pictures, shapes, icons, diagrams and your Lab 3 chart.
Arrange them with corners, cropping, align and group.
Respect the owners and the people in your photos, and add alt text.
Next, in topic 4.5, we'll add gentle motion with transitions
and animations.
