Quick recap

Every cell has an address: column letter + row number.

  • E2 is column E, row 2: the Total Cost of A4 bond paper.
  • Like a chessboard square, the address tells you exactly where to look.
  • A formula like =C2*D2 does not store numbers. It stores directions to other cells.
  • Change the number in C2 and the answer updates by itself.

The big idea

References work like cinema seats.

Relative · C2

"Sit one seat to the right of your friend." Move the friend, and your seat moves too.

Absolute · $C$2

"Row F, Seat 12" printed on the ticket. Whoever holds it, the seat never changes.

The $ sign is a lock, not pesos or dollars. $C locks the column, $2 locks the row.

Relative references · the default

Fill down, and the row numbers follow you.

Microsoft Excel for Mac, sample Lab3 Inventory. Show formulas: Ctrl+` on Windows and Mac, or Formulas › Show Formulas.

  1. Type =C2*D2 in E2. The formula bar keeps the formula; the cell shows ₱6,125.00.
  2. Drag the fill handle (the small square at the cell's corner) down to E9, or double-click it.
  3. Show formulas: E5 became =C5*D5. Each row uses its own Qty and Unit Cost. That's exactly what we want here.

Worked example · 12% VAT for every row

One rate cell. Fill down. Why all the zeros?

Broken

Microsoft Excel for Mac, sample Lab3 Inventory.

  1. Rate 12% in B12. In G2, =E2*B12 → 6,125.00 × 12% = ₱735.00. Row 2 is right.
  2. Fill down to G9: every row below shows ₱0.00, and the VAT total is only ₱735.00.
  3. Look at the formula bar: G3 says =E3*B13. B13 is empty, and empty counts as zero.

What the spreadsheet did

Both addresses moved down one row each time

G2

=E2*B12

6,125.00 × 12% = 735.00

G3

=E3*B13

7,950.00 × blank = 0.00

G4

=E4*B14

960.00 × blank = 0.00

G9

=E9*B19

1,230.00 × blank = 0.00

E should move

Each row needs its own Total Cost, so E2, E3, E4… is right.

B12 should stay

There is only one VAT rate. Every row must keep pointing at B12.

Absolute reference · the fix

Lock the rate: =E2*$B$12

Fixed

Microsoft Excel for Mac, sample Lab3 Inventory.

  1. Click G3: =E3*$B$12. The E part moved; the locked B12 stayed. G3 = 7,950.00 × 12% = ₱954.00.
  2. Every row now uses B12: 735.00, 954.00, 115.20 … 147.60.
  3. Check it: ₱22,039.00 (E10) × 12% = ₱2,644.68. It matches G10, =SUM(G2:G9).

Don't type the $ signs

Press F4 to cycle through the locks

Microsoft Excel for Mac, sample Lab3 Inventory. The formula bar after each press, shown after Enter. G2 stays 735.00 in all four.

Click inside the address in the formula (or just after it), then press F4 again and again:

  1. B12 · relative, nothing locked
  2. $B$12 · absolute, column and row locked
  3. B$12 · mixed, row locked
  4. $B12 · mixed, column locked, then back to B12

Mixed references · in brief

Lock only the row, or only the column.

Microsoft Excel for Mac, sample price list.

  1. Type =$A2*B$1 once in B2.
  2. Fill right and down, and the whole price table fills itself.
  3. C3 reads =$A3*C$1: 85.00 × 5 = 425.00.
  4. $A3: the column stays A (unit costs). C$1: the row stays 1 (quantities).
Good to know: beginners rarely need mixed references. Relative and absolute cover most office sheets.

  • Build formulas by clicking: type =, click the cell, and the address is written for you. Fewer typos.

See it everywhere

One input cell, many rows that use it

At the office
  • 12% VAT on a supplies list: one rate cell, locked with $.
  • Share of the budget: each item's cost ÷ the grand total, =E2/$E$10. A4 paper is 27.8% of ₱22,039.00.
  • Training allowance: days attended × one daily rate cell.
At home
  • Sari-sari markup: selling price = cost × (1 + markup). With 15% in E1: =B2*(1+$E$1). A ₱24 can of sardines sells at ₱27.60.
  • Savings goal: 10% of each payday, one rate cell for all 24 paydays.
  • Kids' allowance: school days × one daily amount.

Recap · Topic 3.5

Decide what moves and what stays.

Relative · E2

Moves when you fill. Use it for "this row's" values.

Absolute · $B$12

Never moves. Use it for one rate or total shared by every row. Press F4.

Mixed · $A2, B$1

Locks only the column or only the row, for two-way tables.
