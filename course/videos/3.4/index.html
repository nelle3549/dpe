Five functions answer most questions about a list

Wrong range, the total row, text in number cells

Remember the long plus formula from topic 3.3?
Now imagine it for 300 rows.
In this video, five short functions do that work for
you: SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT, MIN and MAX.
Together, they answer most questions a section chief asks about
a list, in seconds.
A function works like the Cook button on a rice cooker:
put in the rice and water,
press one button, and it runs all the steps for you.
Read it piece by piece.
Equals means calculate.
SUM is the function's name. The parentheses hold the input.
And E2 colon E9 is the range,
E2 to E9.
Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice Calc all use the same
names.
Each of the five answers one plain question. SUM adds everything:
how much in total?
AVERAGE divides the total by how many numbers:
what's typical?
COUNT counts the cells that hold numbers: how many?
MIN finds the lowest, and MAX finds the highest.
Same pattern for all five: equals, the name, then the range in parentheses.
The fastest way is AutoSum. Delete the long plus formula and click E10.
Press Alt equals.
Excel writes equals SUM of E2 to E9,
and shows the range with a dashed border. Check the range, then press Enter:
22,039 pesos, the same total in one short function.
Do the same for the quantities in C10:
98. In Google Sheets and Calc, use the sigma button.
Below the table, type equals AVERAGE of E2 to E9.
The answer:
2,754 pesos and 88 centavos, the average total cost per
item. On the unit cost column, the average is 250 pesos
and 38 centavos.
Now COUNT of C2 to C9 gives 8.
But COUNT on the item names gives zero, because item names are text.
To count any filled cell, use COUNTA.
MIN and MAX find the extremes.
MIN of D2 to D9 returns 32 pesos:
the staple wire.
MAX returns 520 pesos:
the folder at the top.
But on Total Cost, the highest is long bond paper,
7,950 pesos, because we stock 30 reams.
They return the value, not the item name,
so you still read the table.
At the office, try a daily tally of walk-in clients.
SUM gives 200 clients this week,
an average of 40 a day,
with the busiest day Thursday at 52 and the quietest
Wednesday at 28.
At home, sum your actual spending:
21,210 pesos against a budget of 20,250.
That's 960 pesos over, and groceries are the biggest item.
Watch out for three classic mistakes.
A range that stops one row short misses the envelopes:
20,809 pesos.
A range that includes the total row doubles it:
44,078.
And 30 reams in a quantity cell turns 98 into
68. One habit catches all three:
click the result and read the range.
Does it cover exactly your data rows?
Quick check.
On our inventory, what does equals AVERAGE of E2 to
E9 return?
22,039 pesos, 2,754 pesos and 88 centavos,
250 pesos and 38 centavos, or 8?
Think about it for a moment. The answer: 2,754 pesos and 88 centavos.
That's 22,039 divided by 8 items.
To recap: equals, name, range, and AutoSum writes it for you.
SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT, MIN and MAX answer most questions about
a list. And avoid the three mistakes by reading the range.
