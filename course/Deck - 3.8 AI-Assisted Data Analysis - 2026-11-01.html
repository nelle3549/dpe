The big idea

Treat AI like a fast, eager new OJT.

  • Fast: drafts a formula in seconds.
  • Confident: sounds sure even when it's wrong.
  • New: doesn't know your file, your office rules or what's confidential unless you say so.
  • Supervised: you check the work, because your name is on the report.

Everyone has worked with an OJT or a new hire. Bright, quick, eager to please, and sometimes confidently wrong. You don't stop them from helping; you review their work before it goes out.

Ask: "Would you sign a report an OJT prepared without checking it?" No. Same with AI. The report carries your name, your section's name, and the agency's name.

Vendor-agnostic

Three places you might meet AI for spreadsheets

🟩

Copilot in Excel

The Copilot button on the Home tab opens a side pane. Ask for formulas, summaries or charts about your sheet.

🟦

Gemini in Google Sheets

The Ask Gemini side panel can suggest formulas and summarise data in the open sheet.

Needs a Google Workspace or Google AI plan that includes Gemini.

💬

A chat assistant

Copilot, Gemini, ChatGPT and others in a browser. Describe your columns; don't paste real records.

Works with any app, including LibreOffice Calc, which has no built-in AI.

Office rule first: use only AI tools your office allows, and follow your ICT focal person's guidance. Your office copy may not have these features turned on.

Features and names change often, so focus on the idea: there's a built-in assistant in Excel and Google Sheets if your licence includes it, and there's always a separate chat assistant in the browser.

Built-in assistants can see the sheet you open, so be careful what that sheet contains. With a separate chat assistant, you control exactly what you paste: describe the columns, not the people. Don't promise participants that their office PCs have Copilot; many won't.

Prompting

Name the columns, the rows and the app

✗ Vague

Give me a formula for my inventory.

Which columns? Which rows? Excel or Sheets? The AI has to guess.

✓ Specific

I use Excel. Row 1 has headers: A Item, B Unit, C Qty, D Unit Cost, E Total Cost. Data is in rows 2 to 9. Give me a formula for E2 that multiplies Qty by Unit Cost, and tell me how to copy it down.

1 · Your app2 · Column letters + names3 · Which rows4 · What you want, in plain words

The single biggest improvement: describe the sheet's structure. AI can't see your screen when you use a chat assistant, so tell it the app, the column letters with their names, and which rows hold data.

Notice the good prompt has no real data at all, only headers. That's enough for a formula. Ask: "What would you add to the vague prompt?" Get two or three answers in the chat.

AI assistant · sample conversation
Same headers as before. I also want the grand total in E10, and how many items have a Total Cost above ₱1,000.
AI
In E2: =C2*D2, then drag the fill handle down to E9.
In E10: =SUM(E2:E9)
Items above ₱1,000: =COUNTIF(E2:E9,">1000")

Job 1 · suggest a formula

Paste it in, then check it

  • E10 should show ₱22,039.00.
  • The COUNTIF gives 5. Count by eye: A4, long, folder, ink, envelope. ✓
  • New function? Ask the AI to explain each part before you trust it.

This is a realistic exchange. The first two formulas we already know from 3.3 and 3.4, so we can judge them instantly. COUNTIF is new: it counts cells that meet a condition.

Model the habit: paste it, see 5, then count by eye. Five items have totals above ₱1,000: A4 paper, long paper, folder, ink, envelope. The ballpen at 960 does not. It matches, so we keep it.

AI assistant · sample conversation
A co-worker's file has =SUMIF(B2:B9,"ream",E2:E9). Explain it in simple words.
AI
It looks down the Unit column (B2:B9) for cells that say "ream". For each match, it adds the Total Cost from the same row (E2:E9). So it gives the total spent on items bought by the ream.

Job 2 · explain a formula

Great for inherited files

  • Check the claim: the ream rows are A4 (6,125.00) and long (7,950.00).
  • 6,125 + 7,950 = ₱14,075.00. Does the cell show the same? ✓
  • Ask "explain like I'm new" for any formula you didn't write.

Every office has a spreadsheet built by someone who has since transferred. Explaining formulas is one of the safest, most useful AI jobs: you only share the formula, not the data.

Still verify: the explanation says it adds Total Cost where the unit is "ream." Our two ream items are A4 and long bond paper, 6,125 plus 7,950 equals 14,075.00. If the cell shows that, the explanation holds.

AI assistant · sample conversation
Supplies spending: Jan ₱22,039.00, Feb ₱17,518.00, Mar ₱24,652.00. In January, A4 paper was ₱6,125, long paper ₱7,950. What stands out?
AI
• Bond paper was 63.9% of January spending.
• February fell 20.5% from January.
• March rose 40.7% from February: the highest month of the quarter.

Job 3 · spot trends

Good questions, not final answers

  • Recompute each figure with a formula, e.g. =(B4-B3)/B3 for the March rise.
  • AI sees numbers, not reasons. Why did March rise? Ask the supply officer.
  • Share only totals, never records about people.

Trend-spotting is where AI feels most impressive, and where it's most risky, because percentages are easy to get slightly wrong.

These three are correct: 14,075 out of 22,039 is 63.9%; February is 20.5% below January; March is 40.7% above February. But we only know that because we recomputed. And the AI can't tell you why March jumped, maybe a big printing job. That's human knowledge.

Human verification

Three checks before you trust any AI answer

Takes 2 minutes
🧮

1 · Check 2 rows by hand

Use a calculator on two rows. Row 2: 25 × 245 = 6,125 ✓ Row 9: 3 × 410 = 1,230 ✓

📏

2 · Check the range

Click the cell and read the formula bar. Does it cover E2:E9, all 8 items, and not the TOTAL row?

⚖️

3 · Check against the total

Parts should add up to the whole. Shares should add up to 100%. VAT total = grand total × 12%.

Also ask yourself: does the answer make sense? An average larger than the biggest item, or a share over 100%, is a red flag.

These three checks catch almost every AI mistake, and they work on any formula, not just AI ones.

Two rows by hand, the first and the last, because range errors usually hit the ends. The range in the formula bar. Against the total: add the parts another way and compare. Plus a sense check: our average total cost is ₱2,754.88; if AI said ₱27,000 you'd know instantly something is off.

Hallucinated functions

#NAME? means "I don't know that word."

  • AI sometimes invents a name that sounds real, like SUMVALUES.
  • Some real functions exist only in some apps or versions. XLOOKUP, for example, isn't in Excel 2016.
  • Fix: tell the AI your exact app and version, and ask it to use only built-in functions.
Check fast: type =SUM and look at the function list that pops up. If the name isn't there, it doesn't exist.

The formula bar shows =SUMVALUES(E2:E9), and the cell shows #NAME?. That error means the spreadsheet doesn't recognise the function name. The correct formula is simply =SUM(E2:E9).

A subtler case: a function that's real but newer than your software. Many government PCs run older Office versions. So always include your app and version in the prompt. The autocomplete list is the quickest truth test.

Privacy · non-negotiable

Never upload taxpayer data or confidential records to public AI tools.

OK to share

  • Column headers
  • Formulas
  • Made-up sample rows
  • Overall totals that aren't confidential

Never share

  • Names, TINs, addresses
  • Taxpayer returns or payments
  • Personnel records
  • Screenshots of any of these

Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) · National Privacy Commission (NPC)

This is the most important slide for our office. Whatever you paste into a public AI tool leaves your control. Taxpayer information is confidential, and personal information is protected under the Data Privacy Act of 2012, RA 10173.

The safe pattern: describe the structure with headers, or create fake rows like "Juan Dela Cruz, 123-456-789-000," get the formula, then apply it to the real file yourself. If unsure, ask your data protection officer or ICT focal person first.

Lab 3 · step plan

From raw data to chart in seven steps

  1. Enter or clean the raw data. One header row, no blank rows, numbers typed as numbers (no "₱" or spaces typed in).
  2. Format. Bold headers, Currency (₱) on money columns, a Date column if you have one.
  3. Formulas. Total = Qty × Unit Cost (=C2*D2), filled down.
  4. Functions. =SUM() for the grand total, =AVERAGE() for the average.
  1. Ask AI for one formula (headers only), paste it, and note what you checked.
  2. Verify: 2 rows by hand, the range, and the total.
  3. Chart: a titled Pie, Bar or Line chart from the right range. Save as Lab3_Surname.
Tip: add a small Notes sheet: "AI suggested =AVERAGE(E2:E9). Checked rows 2 and 9 by hand; totals match." Two lines are enough.

Walk the seven steps. Steps 1 to 4 are the spreadsheet itself; step 5 and 6 are the verification part; step 7 is the chart and saving.

On cleaning: the most common raw-data problems are blank rows, numbers typed with a peso sign so they become text, and inconsistent spelling. Remind them to save often with Ctrl + S; a brownout during a 4-hour lab is a real risk. File name example: Lab3_DelaCruz.xlsx.
