Pictures

Insert a picture in three clicks

  1. Go to your slide, click Insert › Image. PowerPoint: Insert › Pictures.
  2. Upload from computer (PowerPoint: This Device) and pick your Lab 1 image.
  3. The picture lands selected, with round handles. Next, size it from a corner.
  • Or drag the file from File Explorer onto the slide.
  • Screenshot: Win + Shift + S, then Ctrl + V.

Google Slides, sample Lab 4 file with a course stock photo. PowerPoint and Impress use the same corner and side handles.

Resize

Drag the corners, never the sides

  1. Click the picture: corner handles and side handles appear.
  2. A side handle stretches it: faces look wide, the office seal looks wrong. Undo with Ctrl + Z.
  3. A corner handle keeps the shape. Hold Shift to be extra safe.
Already stretched? PowerPoint: Picture Format › Reset Picture › Reset Picture & Size. To fill a space, crop instead.

Google Slides, sample Lab 4 file with a course stock photo. (double-click the picture to crop). PowerPoint: Picture Format › Crop. Impress: right-click › Crop.

Crop and compress

Cut out what doesn't help

  1. Click the picture › Picture Format › Crop. Black crop handles appear.
  2. Drag a black handle inward; the greyed part will be cut.
  3. Click outside the picture (or press Enter) to finish.

Extras: Crop to Shape (a circle for a profile photo) and Aspect Ratio (1:1, 16:9).

Compress: Picture Format › Compress Pictures, tick "Delete cropped areas", choose Web (150 ppi) or E-mail (96 ppi). Keep the original photos in a folder.

Google Slides, sample Lab 4 file with a course stock photo. PowerPoint: Insert › Shapes; Impress: Insert › Shape.

Shapes and icons

Simple graphics that point and explain

  1. Insert › Shapes, pick one, then drag on the slide. Hold Shift for a perfect circle or square.
  2. Type inside: click the shape and start typing.
  • Icons: Insert › Icons in Microsoft 365: search "printer", recolour with Graphics Fill. Google Slides and Impress: icons from free sites.
Keep one style: all line icons or all filled icons, in your theme colours.

Google Slides, sample Lab 4 file. PowerPoint: select the bullets › Home › Convert to SmartArt (or Insert › SmartArt). Impress: shapes plus connectors, then group.

SmartArt and diagrams

Turn a list into a diagram

  1. Insert › Diagram › Process. PowerPoint: select your four bullets › Home › Convert to SmartArt.
  2. Set the number of steps (4 modules) and pick a design.
  3. Type the four modules into the shapes.

Match the meaning: Process for steps in order, Cycle for a monthly report, Hierarchy for an org chart.

Align and distribute

Hang your objects like picture frames

  1. Placed by eye, nothing lines up. Select all three with Shift + click.
  2. Align › Middle: the level for your frames.
  3. Now they sit on one line.
  4. Distribute › Horizontally: equal gaps, like a tape measure.

PowerPoint also shows Smart Guides, red dashed lines, while you drag.

Group and arrange

Stack objects like papers on a desk

Back: background shape
Middle: photo
Front: label

Using images legally

Found on Google is not free to use

Your own photos

Safest. Your Lab 1 image, office photos you took (with permission).

Free photo sites

Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay: free to use under each site's licence.

Wikimedia Commons

Many licences; read each one and give the credit it asks for.

Built-in stock

PowerPoint Insert › Pictures › Stock Images (Microsoft 365).

Copyright: almost every photo, logo and cartoon online belongs to someone. Using it without permission can get the office in trouble.
People in photos: a recognisable face is personal data under the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173). Ask colleagues first; never use taxpayers' photos or documents.

Accessibility

Alt text describes the picture

  1. Right-click the picture › Alt text (PowerPoint: View Alt Text).
  2. Type what it shows and what it means, in one or two sentences.

Graphics that help

Pictures and charts, at work and at home

At the office
  • Briefing: the Lab 3 chart with a message title, plus a 3-row table on a backup slide.
  • Orientation for new staff: a SmartArt process of how a document moves; a floor plan drawn with shapes, grouped.
  • Never: photos of taxpayers or screenshots showing a TIN.
At home
  • Family reunion slideshow: crop old photos, compress them, align captions under each one.
  • Household budget talk: a bar chart of Budget vs Actual for rice, CEPALCO, water and load.
  • Sari-sari side business: your own product photos, not ones copied from a brand's page.
