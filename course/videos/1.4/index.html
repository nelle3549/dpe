The law · RA 10173

The Data Privacy Act of 2012

Covers all offices

Government and private. The NPC enforces it.

Sensitive data

Includes government-issued numbers and tax returns

Legitimate purpose

Use data only for official work, only what you need

People's rights

To be informed, to access and correct, to complain

The Tax Code also penalizes revealing taxpayer information without authority.

If it happens · act fast, no shame

Hacked or scammed? Do this.

1

Stop. No more money, codes or replies.

2

Office device or account? Tell IT right away.

3

Change passwords from a safe device. Turn on MFA.

4

Money involved? Call your bank or e-wallet's official hotline.

5

Keep evidence. Report to the CICC hotline 1326.

In this video, you'll learn to spot scams,
protect your accounts, communicate politely online,
and keep taxpayer data safe under the Data Privacy Act.
Scammers don't break into the computer. They trick the person.
Phishing comes by email:
a fake refund and a lookalike login page.
Smishing is the text or chat version, with a short link.
Vishing is a voice call asking for your OTP or
a payment.
The bait is a prize or a fear, and the hook is a link,
a file or a phone number.
Let's spot the red flags in this sample email.
The sender's domain is help desk dot site,
not bir dot gov dot ph. The subject shouts in all caps,
with a 24-hour deadline.
Dear Taxpayer is generic, and forfeited is misspelled.
It asks for your password and OTP.
And the link goes to a lookalike site,
with an attachment ending in e x e. Hover before you click.
Now lock your accounts.
Use a passphrase:
a few random words joined together,
at least 15 characters.
Length matters most.
Use a different one for each important account,
with no birthdays, names or TIN.
Then turn on multi-factor authentication, so a stolen password alone
isn't enough. And never share your OTP.
It's for you to type, never to say.
Six daily habits block most attacks.
Install updates:
they fix security holes.
On public Wi-Fi, no banking or office logins.
Press Windows L every time you stand up.
Keep a clean desk, with no passwords on sticky notes.
Give any unknown flash drive to IT.
And back up important files to the office's approved drive,
never a personal account.
Netiquette is good manners online.
Do write a clear subject and a greeting.
Use Reply All only if everyone needs it.
Check before forwarding:
is it true, and is it okay to share?
And respect work hours.
Don't write in all caps: it reads as shouting.
Never post taxpayer names, TINs or documents in a group
chat, because screenshots travel.
And settle disagreements privately.
Republic Act 10173, the Data Privacy Act,
protects personal information in government and private offices.
The National Privacy Commission enforces it.
Sensitive personal information, like TINs and tax returns,
gets extra protection.
Use data only for a legitimate purpose,
and only what you need.
People have rights:
to be informed, to access and correct their data,
and to complain.
And the Tax Code also penalizes revealing taxpayer information without
authority.
At the office, verify identity before giving any taxpayer information
by phone or chat. Use official email and approved drives only.
No selfies that show your screen.
And shred printouts with personal data.
At home, don't post your kids' school ID or your home address.
Never share your GCash OTP or MPIN.
And teach the family to stop, check and ask.
If it happens, act fast. Stop:
don't send more money, codes or replies.
Office device or account? Report to your ICT focal person right away.
Change your passwords from a safe device, starting with email.
Money involved?
Call your bank or e-wallet through its official hotline.
Keep the evidence, and report to the CICC hotline, 1326.
